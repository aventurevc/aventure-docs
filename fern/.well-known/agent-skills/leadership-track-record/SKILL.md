# Track Record

The deliverable is a verified account of a person's professional record, so a reader deciding on a hire, a board seat, an investment, or a partnership sees what the person did, what happened while they were there, and what remains unverified. It covers professional conduct only.

## Inputs

- The subject: an aVenture person URL or a name. The URL wins when both appear.
- Role or decision it informs (optional): when given, weight the analysis toward evidence that bears on that role.
- Context (optional): facts or constraints the reader supplied.

## 1. Pin the Person

1. Read the subject's aVenture person record before any web search: roles, companies, investments, and news.
2. Disambiguate namesakes before collecting anything. Confirm each source refers to the same person through at least one hard link: the same company and title, a linked profile, or a photo on a first-party page. Drop any source that cannot be linked; people with common names collide often.
3. When two people fit, stop and report both candidates with the fact that separates them.

## 2. Build the Role History

For each role: organization, title, start and end dates, and the source. Prefer company filings, the organization's own pages, and dated announcements over self-published biographies. A role that appears only in the person's own biography is listed and marked self-reported.

## 3. Assess Outcomes

For each significant role, collect measurable events during the tenure: funding raised, revenue or customer milestones, launches, acquisitions (announcement and completion dates apart), layoffs, shutdowns, regulatory actions, and leadership departures.

Attribution is the hard part:
- A result during someone's tenure is not proof they caused it. Credit the person only when a source attributes the result to their role or decision.
- Founders and chief executives carry company-level outcomes; other executives carry outcomes in their function, where sources describe them.
- A title such as "advisor" or "early employee" carries no company outcome without a source describing the contribution.

## 4. Add the Rest of the Record

- Board seats and investments, from the aVenture record and filings.
- Public statements and published work relevant to the role, dated and linked.
- Legal and regulatory matters only from court records, regulator releases, or established outlets reporting on them, with the case status as of the date read. Allegations are labeled as allegations.
- Exclude personal life, family, health, home location, and anything not tied to professional conduct.

## 5. Write the Report

Order:
1. Answer: three to five sentences on the person's verified record as it bears on the reader's decision.
2. Role history table: organization, title, dates, source, verified or self-reported.
3. Outcomes by role, with attribution stated.
4. Board seats and investments.
5. Public record matters.
6. Unverified claims: what the person or others assert that no independent source confirms.
7. Gaps: what could not be sourced.

## Evidence Rules

- Source ladder: filings and court or regulator records, then organizations' own pages and announcements, then dated reporting from established outlets, then the person's own profiles, labeled as self-reported.
- Keep announcement and completion dates apart.
- Cite each sourced fact where it is used.

## Writing Rules

- Each sentence states a fact, a date, a number, or a judgment tied to evidence.
- Never write: "visionary", "seasoned", "proven leader", "serial entrepreneur" without the count, "passionate", "robust", "game-changer", "unlock", "leverage", "delve", "it's worth noting", "in conclusion", or a closing summary that repeats the answer.
- Characterize conduct by what the record shows, not by adjectives.
- State uncertainty once, at the claim it affects, with its cause.
