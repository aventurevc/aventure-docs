# Competitor Analysis

The deliverable compares the subject with its three to six closest rivals on evidence, and ends with where the subject is stronger, at parity, or weaker. A reader should be able to name the rival most likely to take the subject's next customer, and why.

## Inputs

- The subject: an aVenture URL or a name. The URL wins when both appear.
- Competitors to include (optional): every name given is analyzed, even if research suggests it is not a direct rival; in that case analyze it and state the evidence that it serves a different buyer.
- Context (optional): the segment, buyer, or decision the comparison serves.

## 1. Pin the Subject

1. Read the subject's aVenture record before any web search: description, headquarters, founding year, funding rounds, people, news, and research.
2. Name the exact entity or product covered. Parents, subsidiaries, brands, and spin-offs are distinct: Nabisco is a Mondelez brand, not a company; Kellogg Company split in October 2023 into Kellanova and the spun-off WK Kellogg Co. Compare the unit that sells the product, and do not credit a parent's scale to it.
3. When two records fit the name, stop and report both candidates with the fact that separates them.

## 2. Select Competitors

1. A direct competitor sells to the same buyer, for the same job, from the same budget. Candidates come from aVenture's similar-company results, the subject's own comparison pages, buyer review sites, and "alternatives to" searches.
2. Verify each candidate from its own site or documentation. Choose three to six, plus every user-named competitor.
3. List near-misses in one line each with the reason they were excluded.

## 3. Compare

For each dimension, collect the same evidence for every company. When one company's figure is missing, write not disclosed in its cell; never fill it from a different year or a different measure without saying so.

| Dimension | Evidence to collect |
|---|---|
| Product | Capabilities from documentation, changelogs, and API references, not marketing pages; launch dates of the capabilities that matter to the buyer |
| Pricing | Published plans and prices with the date read; packaging and usage limits; where pricing is sales-only |
| Customers | Named customers, segments served, case studies, review volume and rating with the date read |
| Distribution | Sales motion (self-serve, inside sales, field sales, channel), partnerships, marketplaces, geography |
| Traction | Revenue or ARR where disclosed, customer counts, headcount trend, web or app usage from a named source, each dated |
| Funding and backing | Total raised, last round with date, amount, and lead investor, from the aVenture record and announcements |
| Team | Founders and current leaders with relevant prior roles, verified from the aVenture record or primary sources |
| Durable advantages | Network effects, switching costs, proprietary data, regulatory licenses, scale economies, each with the evidence that it exists |

## 4. Judge Relative Strength

For each dimension, rate the subject against each rival as stronger, parity, or weaker, with the one piece of evidence that decides it. Do not invent weighted numeric scores. Then state:

- Where the subject wins and the buyer type for whom that matters.
- Where it loses and what it would take to close the gap.
- The rival moves that would hurt most, each tied to evidence of that rival's direction (hiring, launches, funding, pricing changes).

## 5. Write the Report

Order:
1. Answer: three to five sentences naming the strongest rival, the subject's clearest advantage, and its largest gap.
2. Competitor set with selection reasons and near-misses.
3. Comparison table by dimension, then a short section per dimension where the table needs explanation.
4. Relative strength judgments.
5. What to watch: the dated signals that would change the judgments.
6. Gaps: what could not be sourced.

## Evidence Rules

- Source ladder: filings and regulators, then company primary sources, then dated reporting from established outlets, then analyst estimates labeled as estimates.
- Keep reported, estimated, and computed values visibly separate.
- Keep announcement and completion dates apart; a first close, an announced deal, or a planned feature is labeled as such.
- Cite each sourced fact where it is used.

## Writing Rules

- Each sentence states a fact, a number, a comparison, or a judgment tied to evidence.
- Never write: "best-in-class", "industry-leading", "robust", "seamless", "cutting-edge", "game-changer", "unlock", "leverage", "delve", "it's worth noting", "in conclusion", or a closing summary that repeats the answer.
- Quote a company's own claim only when attributed ("the company says") and checked against another source.
- Numbers carry currency, unit, period, and an as-of date.
- State uncertainty once, at the claim it affects, with its cause.
