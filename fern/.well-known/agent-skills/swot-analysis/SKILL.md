# SWOT Analysis

The deliverable is a SWOT a reader can act on: few items, each specific to the subject, each backed by evidence, each with its consequence stated, and crossed into actions for the decision the reader named.

## Inputs

- The subject: an aVenture URL or a name. The URL wins when both appear.
- Decision it should inform (optional): an investment, partnership, acquisition, competitive response, or job decision. When given, rank every item by its effect on that decision. When absent, rank by effect on the subject's revenue growth.
- Context (optional): constraints or facts the reader supplied.

## 1. Pin the Subject

1. Read the subject's aVenture record before any web search: description, headquarters, founding year, funding rounds, people, news, and research. When the record already holds a SWOT research entry, treat it as a starting hypothesis to verify and date, not as the answer.
2. Name the exact entity covered. Parents, subsidiaries, brands, and spin-offs are distinct: Nabisco is a Mondelez brand, not a company; Kellogg Company split in October 2023 into Kellanova and the spun-off WK Kellogg Co. A strength of the parent is not the subsidiary's strength unless the subsidiary can use it.
3. When two records fit the name, stop and report both candidates with the fact that separates them.
4. For an investment firm, analyze the firm as an investor: fund sizes and vintages, deployment pace, portfolio outcomes, partners, reputation with founders, and access to deals.

## 2. Gather Evidence

Collect before classifying: financial or scale figures with dates, product and pricing facts, customer and review evidence, team history, funding history, competitor moves, market growth figures, and scheduled regulation. Use filings and official sources first, company primary sources next, dated reporting from established outlets after that, and label analyst estimates as estimates.

## 3. Classify

- Strengths and weaknesses are internal: things the subject controls or owns today (product, cost structure, team, customers, balance sheet, distribution, brand).
- Opportunities and threats are external: market, competitor, regulatory, technology, and macro conditions the subject does not control.
- An item that fits two quadrants goes where its cause sits. "A competitor cut prices" is a threat; "our cost per unit is twice theirs" is a weakness.

Each item must be:
1. Specific to this subject. "Strong team", "competition", and "economic uncertainty" are banned unless stated with the subject-specific fact that makes them true (who, what number, which rival, which rule).
2. Evidenced, with its source.
3. Followed by its consequence: what it does to revenue, cost, risk, or the reader's decision.

Keep three to five items per quadrant, ranked by impact. Cut the rest; a long list hides the items that matter.

## 4. Cross Into Actions

Write one line for each pairing that has substance:
- Strength × Opportunity: the move that uses a strength to capture an opportunity.
- Strength × Threat: how a strength blunts a threat.
- Weakness × Opportunity: the opportunity the subject will miss unless a weakness is fixed.
- Weakness × Threat: the exposure where a weakness meets a threat, and its likely cost.

Each action names who acts, what changes, and the metric that would show it worked.

## 5. Write the Report

Order:
1. Answer: three to five sentences naming the item in each quadrant that matters most for the decision, and the resulting judgment.
2. The four quadrants, each a ranked list of items with evidence and consequence.
3. The cross-actions.
4. What would change this view: the dated signals that would move an item between quadrants or off the list.
5. Gaps: what could not be sourced.

## Evidence Rules

- Keep reported, estimated, and computed values visibly separate. A private company's undisclosed revenue is written as not disclosed.
- Keep announcement and completion dates apart; a first close, an announced deal, or a planned product is labeled as such.
- Cite each sourced fact where it is used.

## Writing Rules

- Each sentence states a fact, a number, a comparison, or a judgment tied to evidence.
- Never write: "well-positioned", "poised to", "strong brand" without a measure, "robust", "seamless", "cutting-edge", "game-changer", "unlock", "leverage", "delve", "headwinds and tailwinds", "it's worth noting", "in conclusion", or a closing summary that repeats the answer.
- Numbers carry currency, unit, period, and an as-of date.
- State uncertainty once, at the claim it affects, with its cause.
