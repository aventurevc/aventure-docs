# Portfolio Analysis

The deliverable shows what an investor actually does with its money, measured from its deals, so a founder can judge fit and a limited partner or co-investor can judge behavior. Revealed behavior outranks the investor's own description of itself.

## Inputs

- The subject: an aVenture URL or a name of an investment firm or an individual investor. The URL wins when both appear.
- Sector, stage, or period (optional): when given, analyze that slice and state how it compares with the whole portfolio.
- Context (optional): the reader's purpose, such as raising a round or evaluating a fund.

## 1. Pin the Subject

1. Read the subject's aVenture record before any web search: description, headquarters, founding year, people, news, research, and its investments.
2. Name the exact investor covered. A firm's separate funds, its corporate parent, a partner's personal angel investing, and a similarly named firm are different subjects. A person's investments made through a firm belong to the firm unless a source names the person as the lead.
3. When two records fit the name, stop and report both candidates with the fact that separates them.

## 2. Build the Deal List

1. Start from the aVenture investment records for the subject: each portfolio company, round, date, and amount.
2. Add announced deals the records lack from the firm's portfolio page and dated announcements, and mark each addition with its source.
3. Keep one row per company per round. Record whether the subject led, co-led, or followed, only where a source states it.

## 3. Analyze

- Funds: fund names, sizes, vintages, and close status (first close and final close are different events), from filings or announcements.
- Deployment: deal count by year; stage mix; sector mix; geography mix; median and range of round size; the subject's check size where disclosed.
- Leading: share of deals led, and the stages at which it leads.
- Follow-on behavior: how often it returns in later rounds of its own companies.
- Outcomes: IPOs and acquisitions with announcement and completion dates and sources; shutdowns; later rounds at disclosed valuations. A past acquisition of a portfolio company does not prove its current owner. Undisclosed exit values stay undisclosed.
- Co-investors: the investors that appear most often beside the subject, with counts.
- People: the partners who lead deals, with their sectors, where sources attribute deals to them.
- Thesis: the firm's stated focus against the revealed pattern; name where they diverge.
- Recent activity: deals and fund events in the last 12 months, dated.

## 4. Write the Report

Order:
1. Answer: three to five sentences describing what the investor actually backs, how much, at what stage, and how recently.
2. Funds and capital.
3. Deployment tables by year, stage, sector, and geography, with deal counts.
4. Lead and follow-on behavior, check sizes.
5. Outcomes.
6. Co-investors and deal partners.
7. Stated versus revealed thesis.
8. Recent activity.
9. Gaps: deals, amounts, or outcomes that could not be sourced, and the share of the deal list with undisclosed amounts.

## Evidence Rules

- Source ladder: regulatory filings (for example SEC Form D or Form ADV where they exist), then the investor's and portfolio companies' own announcements, then dated reporting from established outlets, then data providers labeled by name.
- Never compute returns, multiples, or fund performance from incomplete data; report what is disclosed.
- Keep announcement and completion dates apart.
- Cite each sourced fact where it is used.

## Writing Rules

- Each sentence states a fact, a number, a comparison, or a judgment tied to evidence.
- Never write: "founder-friendly" or "value-add" without the evidence, "top-tier", "robust", "seamless", "cutting-edge", "game-changer", "unlock", "leverage", "delve", "it's worth noting", "in conclusion", or a closing summary that repeats the answer.
- Percentages state their denominator ("12 of 40 deals, 30%").
- Numbers carry currency, unit, period, and an as-of date.
- State uncertainty once, at the claim it affects, with its cause.
